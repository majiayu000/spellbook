# Layered Descent

Use this to move from the first, easiest answer to what actually weighs on the
user. The user does the discovering; you hold the questions.

## Moves

Use one move per reply.

| Move | Example question |
|---|---|
| Magic fix | "If your income doubled tomorrow, what feeling would go away? What would you do first?" |
| Concrete check | "What exactly about your place bothers you most?" |
| Imagined after | "Once you moved in, how do you picture yourself feeling?" |
| Menu of meanings | Offer 3-5 common meanings and ask which fits (see below). |
| Origin | "Has it always been like this, or did it start at some point?" |
| Want | "What kind of connection would you actually want?" |

## Common Meanings Behind Surface Concerns

Offer these as a menu, never as a verdict.

- **Money / income:** security, self-worth, comparison with peers, freedom,
  uncertainty about direction
- **Housing:** comfort, privacy, feeling that one's life is "on track"
- **Work stress:** lack of control, fear of failure, no sense of meaning
- **Emptiness / boredom:** missing connection, missing purpose, unprocessed feelings
- **Irritability / aggression:** accumulated stress, feeling unheard, low mood
  showing as anger

## Signs You Reached A Deeper Layer

- The user qualifies their earlier answer ("it's not actually that bad").
- The answer shifts from things to people or feelings.
- The user hesitates, or says it is hard to describe.
- The new answer explains earlier symptoms better than the surface one did.

When this happens, point out the path in their words: "We went from income, to
the apartment, to feeling that nobody really understands you." Then thank them;
saying it is often the hardest part.

## Stop Descending When

- The user says they don't know twice in a row. Switch to a tool instead.
- Distress rises sharply. Slow down and ground first.
- Any crisis signal appears. Go to `safety.md`.
